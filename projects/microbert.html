microBERT

A 4 MB transformer that runs in 12 ms on a Cortex-M7. Built for always-on intent classification on microcontrollers.

What it is

microBERT is a distilled and quantized transformer designed for the tiniest devices that still benefit from understanding language — smart switches, doorbells, hearing aids, industrial keypads. It's small enough to live in flash, fast enough to interrupt-respond, and accurate enough to be useful.

How it works

The recipe is conventional in shape but ruthlessly tuned: a 4-layer student is distilled from a BERT-base teacher, then jointly pruned and quantized to int8 weights with int16 activations. We ship two binaries — a cmsis-nn kernel set for ARM Cortex-M and a portable tflite-micro path for everything else.

Why it matters

On-device inference flips the trust model: data never leaves the device, latency is bounded by silicon rather than radio, and the product keeps working when the network doesn't. Most of the cost of building this was not the model — it was the runtime engineering required to make a transformer behave on a chip with 512 KB of RAM.

Status

Shipped. The model and the runtime are open-source; reach out if you want to discuss deployment on a specific chip.
